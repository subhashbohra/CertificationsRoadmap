Three rules that decide the outcome

01

Single monitor, split screen

Terminal left, kubernetes.io docs right. Never practise on two screens. The exam gives you one, and re-learning the layout under time pressure is exactly where the minutes went last time.

02

Never hand-write YAML

Generate every manifest. Set the aliases at the start of every session, not just before the exam.

# set these first, every session
alias k=kubectl
export do="--dry-run=client -o yaml"
export now="--force --grace-period=0"

k run nginx --image=nginx $do > pod.yaml
k create deploy web --image=nginx --replicas=3 $do
03

Patterns, not problems

The win condition for DSA is naming the pattern inside 60 seconds — sliding window versus two pointers versus heap. Five hundred ungrouped problems without that recognition step produces nothing at interview.

Consulting track — parallel, low intensity

Read before signing anything

The original transcript framed an offshore B2B entity as a way to keep outside consulting invisible to your employer. Structuring for non-detection is not the same as being compliant, and financial services is the worst possible sector in which to test that distinction.

Deutsche Bank almost certainly requires written pre-approval for outside business activity, and that obligation attaches to you, not to whichever entity signs the contract. Routing income through an LLP doesn't dissolve it — it just adds a paper trail that reads badly if it's ever examined.

The correct sequence: read your employment contract and the conflicts / outside-business-activity policy, then either get written approval through the proper channel or wait. Advisory-network calls and disclosed side work are often permitted. Undisclosed competing work in your own domain generally isn't. Speak to an employment lawyer in India before you sign, not after.

None of this kills the plan. It sequences it: credentials → higher-paying role or clean exit → consulting entity. Which is broadly what the 26 weeks above already do.
